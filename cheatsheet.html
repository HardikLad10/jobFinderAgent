Booth cheatsheet

Say this like a person, not like a resume. Numbers stay in your pocket until they ask how you know it worked.

The 20-second pitch

Say this

Hi, I’m Hardik. I’m a master’s student at Illinois, graduating in May, and I’m looking for new-grad software engineering roles — also forward deployed or applied AI if that’s how your team is built.

I’ve been an engineer in production, not only in class. What I like is sitting with how people actually work, then owning the software that lives in that workflow — store teams, course staff, students who need materials they can actually use.

I’m less interested in a brand name than in a team where I can grow as someone who ships with other people. What does that first year look like here?

If they stay with you

Add this, not more metrics

A lot of my work has been translating a messy real process into something a team can trust. At Piramal I sat with e-commerce and modern-trade folks who were buried in claims and artwork approvals. The job wasn’t “build a form.” It was stay with the process until the tool became how they worked.

At Illinois I’ve been doing the same kind of thing from two sides: helping business students ship real systems, and making course files usable for people on screen readers. That’s the engineer I want to keep being.

Why this is the pitch

Do not open with 70%, $50K, 100 events/sec, or “2 years experience.” Those are proof, not a reason to talk to you. Lead with judgment, who you help, and the kind of teammate you are. If they lean in, then give one number.

The one-line “why you”

Finish with something true about them, not a compliment about the logo. Steal one of these and swap in what you just read on the booth:

  • “I’m interested because you’re building software that sits next to a real operation, not only a dashboard.”
  • “I’m interested because new grads here seem to own a slice, not only shadow.”
  • “I’m interested because your team has to care about the person on the other side of the tool.”
  • “I’m interested because this is Midwest / Illinois work I can actually picture myself in.”

If they push on the degree

My degree is Information Management. My work has been software engineering. I was a production engineer before I came here, and I’ve kept building systems since — I came to this fair because I want to keep doing that on a team, not because I’m shopping majors.

Stories — pick one that matches them

People and process

Use for FDE, product, internal tools, ops-heavy companies

I get energy from the unglamorous part: watching someone do the job, noticing where the software fights them, and staying until it doesn’t. That’s the Piramal claims / approval work, and it’s also the accessibility work — the user is not hypothetical.

Teaching and taste

Use for teams that talk about mentorship, culture, or AI-in-the-workflow

I TA’d business students through Git, CI, and using AI tools without turning their project into slop. I like making other people more dangerous with the stack. That’s also how I want to be managed: high trust, real feedback.

Systems, if they go technical

Use for platform, infra, or “tell me about a hard problem”

RainStorm was me caring about correctness under failure — partitioning, recovery, not losing work when a node dies. I can go deep there if they want. Don’t volunteer the throughput number unless they ask what “worked” meant.

Pocket numbers — only if they ask

  • Legal-doc RAG / ETL at BIG: people stopped waiting on searches that used to crawl.
  • Piramal: claims and artwork tools the business actually adopted; shelf-audit work that replaced a grind.
  • Accessibility: a couple hundred course files, twenty-plus courses, a small team, real students on assistive tech.

Say the outcome in English first. The percentage is optional punctuation.

Questions that sound like you

  • What does a new grad own in the first six months — a feature, a customer, a system?
  • When a new hire is thriving here, what are they doing that isn’t on the job description?
  • Where does an engineer here sit relative to the user — tickets, shadowing, on-site?
  • How do you decide someone is ready for more scope?
  • What’s the interview timeline after today?

Sponsorship, if it comes up

I will need sponsorship. I’d rather be straightforward now than waste your process. Are you able to hire new grads who need it?

Then stop talking. Let them answer. If no, thank them and leave a resume anyway only if they offer.

Close

This was useful — thank you. What’s the best way to follow up, and is there someone on the team you’d want me to apply to by name?

Write the name down before you leave the booth.

Target: new-grad / intern SWE, FDE, AI Engineer. Not Solutions Engineer.
